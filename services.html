Operational & process excellence

Operational & Process Excellence: IT Operations Transformation

For: Organizations whose outages, backlogs, or service levels are unpredictable, or whose operation works but needs to perform measurably better.

What happens

  • Stabilize: 24x7 monitoring, tiered triage, production change guardrails, and an owner for every service
  • Standardize (process excellence): ITSM and ITIL discipline, SOPs, a service catalog, and baselined, published SLAs and KPIs
  • Automate: ticket analysis to size the opportunity, alert correlation and routing, proactive ticketing
  • Modernize: modern work-tracking, disaster recovery, and a secure development lifecycle
What you get: an operation with named owners and measurable SLAs, a shrinking backlog and incident count, and a team that holds the gains. See the IT operations turnaround.
Assessment & roadmap

Technology Assessment & Transformation Roadmap

Offered as the Delivery Confidence Scan.

For: Leaders who suspect delivery is less predictable than the reports say, or who need an independent, fact-based baseline before committing to a larger program.

What happens (2 to 3 weeks, fixed scope)

  • Review of incident, backlog, and SLA data, plus interviews with the people closest to the work
  • Assessment of the operating model, tooling, and vendor performance
  • Sample ticket analysis to size the automation opportunity
What you get: a plain-language findings readout, a prioritized and sequenced roadmap, baseline metrics with targets, and a recommended next step. If the next step isn’t me, I’ll say so.
Vendors & economics

Vendor Performance & Technology Economics

For: Leaders who need vendors held to the contract, or who face build-versus-buy and fixed-bid-versus-time-and-materials decisions.

What happens: I review SOWs and commercial structure, set up vendor governance and performance measures, and analyze total cost, with the perspective of both the buyer and the delivery partner.

What you get: a clear view of what each vendor is accountable for, a governance cadence, and a total-cost analysis that supports the decision.

Portfolio rationalization, platform modernization, Secure SDLC, and integration. Start here when you are changing core technology and the business cannot afford disruption.

Portfolio strategy

Application Portfolio Assessment & Rationalization

For: Leaders who need to know what they run, what it costs, and what to keep, consolidate, or retire, whether the driver is cost pressure, an acquisition, a modernization program, or audit and risk.

What happens (fixed scope)

  • Inventory the portfolio and map each application to the business capability it supports
  • Assess business value and technical fit on one consistent scale
  • Build a total-cost-of-ownership view for each application
  • Decide a disposition for every application, and sequence the work into funded waves
What you get: a capability map, a fit assessment, a total-cost baseline, a disposition for every application, a multi-wave investment roadmap, and an executive readout built to win a funding decision. See the 350-application case study.
IT modernization

IT Modernization

Core platform migrations are offered as CoreShift.

For: Organizations moving a core system of record (claims, enrollment, billing, policy, or ledger platforms, for example) off a legacy system, or modernizing while keeping a regulated business running.

What happens

  • Readiness and risk assessment across vendors, interfaces, and data
  • Go-live options analysis, such as phased enablement versus a controlled run-out and clean cutover
  • A vendor testing process and change control that work across a multi-vendor ecosystem
  • Interface sequencing and data-cutover rules the business can test before it goes live
What you get: a defensible go-live recommendation, a test-and-cutover plan, and an owner for the process that protects customers and members during the switch. My direct migration experience is on core healthcare platforms.
Secure SDLC

Secure SDLC & DevSecOps

For: Organizations that need security, quality, and audit evidence built into how software is delivered, not bolted on at the end.

What happens

  • Baseline current delivery maturity and security controls
  • Design a secure SDLC: secure coding standards, automated checks in the pipeline, and release gates
  • Put a secure CI/CD toolchain and cloud security guardrails in place
  • Define roles, metrics, and the evidence trail an auditor will ask for

My role: I design the standard and govern its adoption; your engineers implement it.

What you get: a secure SDLC standard, a pipeline control map, a sequenced roadmap, and delivery and security metrics. See related results.

Executive leadership

Fractional & Interim CIO and CTO-Level Leadership

For: Organizations with a leadership gap, a stalled program, or a delivery operation that needs an executive to own it during a period of change.

How it works

  • Fractional: one to three days a week on a monthly retainer, with a weekly sponsor meeting, a delivery rhythm, and a decision log.
  • Interim: full-time coverage of the seat while a permanent leader is found, with a hand-off plan built in.
  • Remote, or on site in the Atlanta area by arrangement.

What I own: technology strategy and roadmap, transformation governance, delivery operating cadence, SLAs and KPIs, vendor governance, executive and board reporting, and team structure.

What I don’t do in this role: hands-on implementation, or engagements without a clear, measurable objective. I lead and restructure delivery; I don’t take the keyboard.

What you get: a stable operation, measurable SLAs, and a team that can run without me.
Innovation & AI

Innovative Solutions & Optimization through AI

For: Organizations that want to use automation and AI to optimize operations, safely and within regulatory and security constraints.

Delivery model

How engagements are delivered

I personally lead every engagement and remain accountable for its outcomes. The people doing the work are usually yours.

  • What I lead: the assessment, the roadmap, the operating cadence, the decisions, the vendor governance, and the reporting to your executives and board.
  • What your team executes: day-to-day operations and implementation work. I set the standard and coach to it, because change that your people helped build is the change that holds after I leave.
  • Specialist capacity: where an engagement needs implementation, engineering, or sector-specific expertise, it comes from your own teams or your chosen partners, and I govern it on your behalf.
  • Scale: RightVector is a boutique practice. It brings senior executive attention rather than the headcount of a systems integrator.
  • iOS development: separate from advisory work, I build native iOS apps hands-on. It is scoped as its own engagement.
